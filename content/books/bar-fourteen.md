@piano
When I was nine, we got a piano.

It was secondhand and slightly out of tune. So was I.
---
@clock
I practised every day after school. The same piece.

The same mistake, in the same place.
---
@glasses
Mrs Lindqvist lived downstairs.

Very old, very tall, and she never smiled in the lift.
---
@piano
One afternoon there were three knocks on the floor, from below.

I stopped playing and waited to be in trouble.
---
@envelope
The next morning there was a note under our door, in tidy blue handwriting.
---
@note
# Bar fourteen is a B flat.
==Keep going.==

~ Mrs L, flat 2
---
@piano
I played it as a B flat. It sounded right for the first time.

The floor stayed quiet.
---
@note
After that, a note every few weeks.

Slower in the middle. Softer left hand. You rush the ending, and it is the best part.
---
@chairs
Years later I played a small concert in a church hall. Forty chairs, mostly parents.

In the back row, very tall, not smiling, sat Mrs Lindqvist.
---
@chairs
Afterwards she shook my hand.

> You still rush the ending.

At the door she turned round.

> It is still the best part.
